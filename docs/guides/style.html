Cinematic · Style

One look for the whole interface

The other hubs say when things show. The Style hub says how they look: your unit frames, nameplates, names, cast bars, tooltips, bubbles, the Visions' cards and the letterbox, all in one theme you can pick in a click and change as much as you like.

Finding it

How the hub is laid out

Open Cinematic's settings (/cine config) and pick Style. At the top is General, with its tabs. Below it the look pages come in three bands, each named after the hub whose frames it dresses:

InterfaceCombat: Unit Frames (with its Cast Bars tab), Personal Resources, Raid Frames, Action Bars and Combat Text. Nameplates: Nameplates (with its Cast Bars tab) and Names. Info: Chat Bubbles and Tooltips.
VisionsAll Visions, then Combat: Damage and Vitals; World: Quest (Dialogue and Quest Item Button) and Roll; RP: Emote and Card Types.
AtmosphereTint and Letterbox.

Each look page is also a tab on its own frame's icon in the other hubs: the Interface hub's Unit Frames icon has its Style tab, a Vision's page has a Style tab beside it, and a small palette by a page's switch takes you to its look. Change it from either place: it's the same page.

Most look pages work the same way: an Enable Page switch (off: the game's own look, your settings kept), a preview at the top drawn by the same code as the real thing, and rows (a Default row, then one per frame or kind) whose Look button opens a panel with that row's settings. A row follows Default until you give it its own look. A setting the theme picked says Set by theme; one that follows General says General's.

The master switch

General

Every game version

Style's General page holds Enable Style, one switch for every Style page at once: off, everything goes back to the game's look, and every page keeps its settings for when you turn it on. Under it, a large preview of a unit frame, nameplates and panels in your current look, and a strip with the theme you're using (click it to go to Themes).

Then the list of pages, a tab for each band, showing what each one goes by:

Theme tickWhen ticked, picking a theme changes this part (frames, panels, action bars, nameplates, names, cast bars or Visions). Untick it to keep a part as it is whatever theme you pick.
Follows General / From the theme / Its ownWhether the page's font, bar texture, outline, shadow and border follow Quick Settings, come from the theme, or have been set on the page itself (Its own: names which). Visions follow All Visions instead.
Use GeneralPuts a page's shared settings back on General, or as the theme set them. The page's name opens it.
A whole look in one pick

Themes

Every game version

The Themes tab (General's second tab) has its own row of tabs: Themes, Quick Settings, Interface Recolor and Overrides. A theme is a whole look for the addon: unit frames, bars, fonts, panels, nameplates and names, cast bars, Visions, and even the colors and art of Cinematic's own menus. A theme only fills in settings, so anything it sets you can still change by hand afterwards; the theme then shows as with your changes. Switching themes with changes made asks first: Save as a new theme, or Switch anyway.

Themes of your own

Under Yours, + New theme makes a theme from the one you're using, with your changes. Right-click any theme for more: Use, Copy to yours, and on yours Save changes, Rename, Description, Share and Backups.

  1. Pick the built-in theme closest to what you want and make it yours. Started from is the theme whose art, shapes and fonts it keeps.
  2. Give it your Colors: Accent (titles, names, menu highlights), Panels, Lines, Rings (portrait rings and corner art), Health, Mana and Casts. Right-click a color to put it back.
  3. Change fonts, bars and shapes on Quick Settings or any Style page, then Save changes.

Your themes are kept for every profile on your account, up to 20 of them. Share gives a line of text for a friend, who adds it with Import; Export shares the theme you're using with your changes. Backups brings back an older copy of a theme of yours, or one you deleted. Reapply theme puts every setting back as the theme has it.

Over the Show rows

Overrides

Hide or show parts of unit frames, nameplates and action bars, whatever their rows on the Interface pages say. A tab each for You, Target (focus and target of target go with it), Your pet, Nameplates and Action bars; each part is Interface page (as its row says), Hide or Show. Drop the portrait, the name background or the button dividers in one place, and the Interface row shows Overridden: hidden while its own ticks wait underneath. Themes use this too. Clear overrides puts every part back on its row.

Interface · Combat

Unit Frames

How your player, target, focus, pet, target of target and party frames look. A Default row, then a row per frame set to Default row's or Its own look. The Look panel has the bars' fill, health color (the game's, class or reaction, custom, or By health, shifting through three colors as health drops), gradients, the name's font, size and color, the Portrait (their face or a class icon) and its Shape: the game's round frame, Circle, Square, Hexagon, Diamond, Arch, Cut square, Eye, Octagon, Shield, Leaf, Crystal, or none at all for just the name and bars. Which parts show at all is the Interface hub's Unit Frames page. When the game draws your party as raid frames, the Party row points to Raid Frames.

Personal Resources

The bars under your character: their fill, border and background, the health color (the game's, class or custom), gradients, each bar's width and height, and your class resource's size. On retail it also dresses the Cine Mana Bar, the mana bar Cinematic adds when mana isn't your shown power (turned on from the Interface hub's Personal Resources page). When the bars show: Frames.

Raid Frames

The raid, party (raid-style) and arena frames: a Default row and a row per group with its own look, health fill and color (By health too), gradient, what's behind the bar, border, and the names' font, size and color. Raid frames keep their class colors in every theme, so you can tell your group apart. No Arena row on WoW Forever, which has no arena frames.

Interface · Nameplates

Nameplates

The nameplates' look, in two parts. The first has a Plate theme strip: one made from each main theme, plus Clean, Minimal and The game's; Follow General (to start) takes Quick Settings' bar texture and border. A plate theme you pick here stays when you change the main theme. Customize changes the plates' basic look, and any Smart Style row can have a look of its own: the bar's texture, color and gradient, the name's font, size, outline and shadow, the border, and on WoW Forever and Classic the level box.

Smart Style is a list of rows (your target, focus, mouseover, aggro on you, elite NPCs, players and the rest): each plate takes its look from the first row that fits it, top to bottom. Each row has a Mark over the plate, a Look for the plate itself (its color, size, opacity) and When it applies. In dungeons and raids the game doesn't let addons change friendly plates. The full tour of the rows is in Nameplates and names.

Interface · Nameplates

Names

How names look on plates that show just the name. A name theme strip first: one per main theme, plus Bold, Subtle, Storybook, Subtitle (white on a soft dark band, like film subtitles), Lantern and The game's. As with plate themes, one picked here stays when the main theme changes.

Then names by kind: a Default row every name starts from, and a row for your target, friendly players (group members and their minions under it), enemy players, hostile, neutral and friendly NPCs, and service NPCs, with a row for each kind of service (vendors, repair, trainers, flight masters, innkeepers, quest givers...). Each row follows the one it's under until you give it its own look:

Text and ColorFont, size and opacity; color by how they see you, class, or one color; friendly players blue, in class colors or white.
Glow and BandA soft light behind the name, or a band of color with a line round it.
Letters and NPC titlesThe guild or title line under the name: its font, size and color. Service NPCs' titles can show as an icon (an anvil for repairs), with or without < >.
Distance and timingHide delay, fade time, Raise, and their size close to and far from the camera (or one size at any distance).

Chat Bubbles

Two tabs. Bubbles: the font, size and outline, what's behind the words (the game's bubble, Dark, Custom, or just the words), its edge, opacity and the tail. Behind Bubbles: Speech from behind you, says and yells shown low on the screen when their bubble is off it, with who said it, which way the lines slide, their size and where they sit (Move). Tooltips

The game's tooltips: the health bar (texture, under the tooltip or along the top, color, gradient, height), the font and text size, names in class or reaction colors, the edge (plain or in the unit's or item's color), and the background's color and opacity.

Visions

The Visions' looks

All Visions is the look every Vision shares: font, outline, shadow, text size, bar texture and border, and which Visions follow it (Use Visions Style puts one back). Each Vision then has its own page:

DamageDamage Vision's bar: texture, colors (Behind and ahead, your class, or your own two), border and height. It reads the game's own damage meter, so only does something where the game has one. See Visions.
VitalsVitals Vision's edges: a theme (Auto picks one for your class and spec, More themes has the rest), its sound, strength, reach, darkness, colors and heartbeat. Make, copy, share and import themes of your own. The themes built from the game's own full-screen effects are retail only.
Quest: DialogueHow NPC dialogue looks: Parchment, Chat bubble, Dark or None; the NPC's portrait, scale, bubble height, where the quest card goes, the font, and number keys and Space to reply.
Quest: Quest Item ButtonThe big quest item button: size, shape, frame, a glow when it's ready, how the cooldown shows, and its key binding in the corner.
RollThe roll window: cards on the game's frame, Flat or nothing, Roomy or Compact rows, the time-left bar, picks as icons or words (Need, Greed, Transmog in their colors), and how the winner's row is lit. Where the game keeps a loot history.
EmoteEmote Vision's card: its style, words, frame, the portrait, its scale and where it sits.
Card TypesThe card types every card starts from: change one and every card using it changes. Each says which cards use it, and how many settings you've changed.
Atmosphere

Tint and Letterbox

Tint is the color grading over the world: pick a look (zone moods and the time of day together, either alone, a single color, or none), make tints of your own, set the strength, the color of each part of the day, and each zone's or area's mood. Letterbox is the bars' look: their height as a share of the screen or the shape of a film's frame (2.39:1, 2:1, 1.85:1), color, opacity, a soft feathered edge, a line along it, and whether they slide or fade in. Where and when each shows, and the rest of the scene: Look and mood.

Questions

Good to know

I picked a theme and now I want the game's look back.
Pick The game's on Themes, or turn off Enable Style on General to set every page aside without losing anything.
Will picking a theme undo my nameplate or name theme?
No. A plate or name theme picked on its own page stays. To keep any other part as it is, untick its Theme tick on General.
I changed a few things. Are they lost if I switch themes?
You're asked first. Save as a new theme keeps them under Yours, for every profile on your account.
Interface Recolor does nothing.
It tints in the theme's color, so it needs a theme picked: with The game's, everything keeps the game's colors.
Can I share my look?
Share a theme of yours as a line of text, or share your whole profile: see Sharing profiles.
