Cinematic · Shoulder Cam

Over your shoulder

The camera sits over your shoulder, with you off to one side of the screen, like a third-person adventure. Cinematic keeps you on your spot as you zoom, eases out of the way when it should, and lets your cast bar and resources slide along with you.

Getting started

Turning it on

WoW ForeverRetail

It's the Shoulder Cam page in the Camera hub. Switch it on and Cinematic asks once: it uses one of Blizzard's experimental camera settings, and the game would otherwise warn about that every time you log in. While it's on, the game's Keep Character Centered and motion sickness options are turned off, because they stop it. Turn the Shoulder Cam off and all your own camera settings come back.

On WoW Forever, the Shoulder Cam profile turns it on for you, with a layout made for it: your resources, cast bar and Cooldown Manager at the bottom middle, sliding along with you.

The Shoulder Cam page

Shoulder

Show inWhere it's on. To start with: not indoors, not in a dungeon, raid or other instance, and not mounted. Everywhere else it puts the camera over your shoulder; tick Always to have it everywhere.
Left shoulder, and the swap key beside itUnticked, the camera looks over your right shoulder and you sit left of the middle. The key flips sides; bind it here or in the game's Key Bindings (Cinematic, Swap shoulders (Shoulder Cam)).
Spot on screenHow far off the middle you sit, as a share of the way to the edge: 20% to start with, 5% to 40%. The camera moves as you zoom to keep you there, and eases you back to the middle when you zoom right in. Zoomed far out it stops moving further, so a wall pulling the camera in can't throw you off screen.
Swap takesHow long the camera takes to cross to the other shoulder: 1.5 seconds to start with. At 0 it's the game's own quick swap.
Back from targeting yourselfWith yourself targeted the game puts you in the middle, and nothing can change that. This is how long the way back over your shoulder takes as you clear the target: 1 second to start with.
Off and on again takesHow long the camera takes to go behind you as you go indoors, mount or leave where Show in has it, and to come back after: 0.9 seconds to start with.
Turning

Looking at who matters

Turn to NPCs you talk toAs you talk to an NPC (a quest giver, vendor, banker, trainer or flight master), the camera turns slowly to bring them toward the middle, and back as you close the window. NPC turn takes, NPC turn back takes and Look at them for set its pace.
Turn to enemyAs you target an enemy, the camera turns slowly toward it, and glides back once you clear it. It has a Show in row of its own: out of fights to start with (the rule not combat). Enemy turn takes sets its pace.

It only turns to enemies. Targeting a friendly guard or another player doesn't turn the camera; talking to an NPC does.

Frames

Frames that follow you

With you off to the side, your cast bar and Personal Resource Display would sit in the middle of the screen, away from you. Frames follow you, on the page's Frames tab, slides them over to where you are, and back as the camera goes behind you. Edit Mode still places them; this moves them from there.

Frames follow the turnAdds to Frames follow you: as Turn to enemy turns the camera, the frames slide along too. Worked out from where you clicked, so it's a little experimental.
Which framesA tick each: cast bar, Personal Resource Display, swing timers, the Cooldown Manager's rows, tracked buffs and spell alerts. A frame placed on a ticked one in Edit Mode slides with it.
Height (all), Follow strengthRaise the frames while the camera's over your shoulder (each also has its own height on its row), and slide them further or less far if they land short of you or past you.
Cameras

When Cinematic's own cameras play

The Cameras tab has a tick for each of Cinematic's cameras. Unticked, the camera goes back behind you while that one plays and comes back after; ticked, you keep your spot as the camera turns round you. Beside each, the seconds the move takes.

Ticked to start withThe walk and auto-run cameras.
Unticked to start withVista, fish, cozy, AFK, tele (Hearthstone and teleports), flight, death, quest and chat cameras, and your own cameras.
Calibrate

Sitting in the right place

How far the offset moves you depends on your character's size. Cinematic knows the races, but if you sit too far to the side or too close to the middle (in a form, say), the Calibrate tab measures you: stand still on open, flat ground and it says a line in /say a few times while it reads where you are. Share sends us the numbers so everyone gets them.

Questions

Good to know

Why does the camera jump to the middle when I target myself?
The game does that, and while you're your own target it ignores the shoulder offset completely. When you clear the target, the camera glides back over Back from targeting yourself.
It doesn't work with the gamepad UI.
Not yet: in gamepad mode the game holds your character in the middle. The Shoulder Cam waits behind you and comes back when the gamepad UI is off.
I use DynamicCam.
DynamicCam sets the same shoulder offset, so the Shoulder Cam stands aside for it.
How do I go back to my old camera?
Turn the Shoulder Cam off. Every camera setting it changed is put back as you had it.
